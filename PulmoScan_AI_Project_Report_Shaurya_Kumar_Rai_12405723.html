1. Abstract & Project Scope

Lung neoplasms represent the highest cause of global oncological mortality, resulting in approximately 1.8 million annual deaths. Although Low-Dose Computed Tomography (LDCT) thoracic imaging is clinically proven to improve patient survival via early intervention, the sheer volume of axial CT slices imposes cognitive fatigue on radiologists, causing diagnostic delays and missed micro-nodules.

This project presents PulmoScan AI, an end-to-end deep learning framework trained to classify thoracic CT images into three classes: Normal Tissue, Benign Nodules, and Malignant Neoplasms. Using transfer learning with a Deep Residual Network (ResNet-18) on the IQ-OTH/NCCD dataset (14,819 CT scans), combined with Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM), the system provides transparent spatial localization of lesions. The model achieved a peak validation accuracy of 91.90%, an overall test accuracy of 88.10%, and a crucial 100.0% recall on malignant cases, completely eliminating false negatives on cancer predictions.

This project was directly conceptualized and implemented from key peer-reviewed research papers in medical imaging, residual transfer learning, and Explainable AI:

Paper 1: Benchmark Dataset & Problem Formulation
H. F. Al-Yasriy, M. S. Al-Husieny, F. Y. Mohsen, E. A. Khalil, and Z. S. Hassan (2020), IOP Conf. Series: Materials Science and Engineering.
Core Contribution & Influence: Established the IQ-OTH/NCCD Lung Cancer Dataset collected from Iraq-Oncology Teaching Hospital & National Center for Cancer Diseases. It proved that deep convolutional networks can differentiate between benign granulomas and malignant carcinomas directly from axial CT slices. This paper provided the benchmark 3-class problem formulation adopted in our project.
Paper 2: Deep Residual Learning for Image Feature Extraction
Kaiming He, Xiangyu Zhang, Shaoqing Ren, and Jian Sun (2016), IEEE CVPR, pp. 770–778.
Core Contribution & Influence: Introduced residual skip connections (y = F(x) + x) to eliminate vanishing gradients in deep networks. While earlier models like VGG-19 require 140M+ parameters and suffer from overfitting on medical scans, ResNet-18 achieves high feature discriminability with only 11.2 million parameters, allowing fast clinical inference and high generalization.
Paper 3: Explainable AI & Visual Interpretability via Grad-CAM
Ramprasaath R. Selvaraju et al. (2017), IEEE ICCV, pp. 618–626.
Core Contribution & Influence: Formulated Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping to compute gradient flows into the final convolutional layer. This paper provided the mathematical foundation implemented in our gradcam.py module, converting a black-box neural network into a transparent clinical diagnostic tool.
Paper 4: Transfer Learning Baseline Comparison (VGG-19)
S. Jaya Prakash, J. Panuganti, P. Rokati, S. Das, G. Lavanya, and N. S. Dey (2024), IJSRSET.
Core Contribution & Influence: Explored VGG-19 transfer learning on the same IQ-OTH/NCCD dataset. Our work expanded upon this reference by upgrading to a lighter ResNet-18 backbone, building a live Grad-CAM visualizer, and deploying an interactive Streamlit clinical application.

3. Dataset Specifications & Clinical Pathology Classes

The system was trained and evaluated on 14,819 clinically labeled axial thoracic CT scans from the IQ-OTH/NCCD collection:

  • Malignant Cases (5,640 scans - 38.1%): Characterized by invasive lobulation, irregular spiculated margins, and high attenuation.
  • Benign Cases (5,019 scans - 33.9%): Characterized by well-circumscribed nodules, smooth borders, and non-invasive granulomas.
  • Normal Cases (4,160 scans - 28.1%): Clear pulmonary parenchyma without pathological lesions or structural distortion.

4. System Architecture & Deep Learning Pipeline

The diagnosis pipeline proceeds in five coordinated stages:

  1. Input Preprocessing: Axial CT slices are resized to 224×224 pixels and normalized with ImageNet mean (μ=[0.485, 0.456, 0.406]) and standard deviation (σ=[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]).
  2. Feature Extraction Backbone (ResNet-18): Residual blocks extract hierarchical spatial primitives (edges, textures, nodule density gradients) across 4 residual stages.
  3. Classification Head: Adaptive Average Pooling compresses feature maps into a 512-D vector, followed by Dropout (p=0.3) and a Linear dense layer mapping to 3 output logits.
  4. Grad-CAM Spatial Backpropagation: Gradients of the predicted class score with respect to layer4[1].conv2 activations are pooled to compute weight importance coefficients (αk), generating a localized heatmap overlay.
  5. Clinical Decision Support Dashboard: Real-time inference displayed in a Streamlit application with confidence distribution bar charts and automated radiology report export.

6. Conclusion

PulmoScan AI establishes that a lightweight Deep Residual Network (ResNet-18) augmented with Explainable AI (Grad-CAM) can perform rapid, accurate, and interpretable thoracic CT triage. Achieving 100% recall on malignant cases and a peak validation accuracy of 91.90%, the system provides a trustworthy second opinion for radiologists, reducing fatigue while accelerating cancer screening.
